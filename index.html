Why Me

Plenty of developers know this stack. Here's what I do with it.

Angular, Laravel, PostgreSQL, Docker — a lot of people can list these. The difference shows up after the demo works, when the system is live, someone's depending on it, and something breaks at 4pm on a Tuesday.

01

I stay for production, not just the demo.

On the Guardian EMR platform I didn't just ship features — I actively tracked production performance and corrected operational problems as they surfaced, on a system holding real patient data. Most developers hand off at deployment. I treat launch as the start of the job, not the end of it.

02

I think in security and AI, not just syntax.

A postgraduate diploma in AI paired with a diploma in Cyber Security means I'm not bolting security on after the fact or treating AI features as a black box — I'm reasoning about attack surface, data handling, and model behavior while the architecture is still being decided.

03

I've sat across from the people who use what I build.

Supporting valueloads.com meant direct, ongoing liaison with brokers, managers, and Accounts Payable/Receivable staff — not a ticket queue, a relationship. I've had to explain a bug to someone who isn't technical and still needs their day to work. That changes how you build things.

04

I leave teams better than I found them.

I've trained and mentored three interns and a junior developer on coding standards and Git workflow — not because I was asked to have that title, but because a codebase only stays healthy if the people writing it are improving too.

Where I'm headed

To be direct about it: I'm not trying to become an AI Engineer or a dedicated Security Analyst — both are deep, specialized fields with their own hiring bars, and I respect that too much to claim I'm already there. What I am is a full-stack developer who understands both well enough that it changes how I build — the AI feature I ship is less likely to become a black box, and the system I hand off is less likely to need someone else to keep it safe. That combination, not a title change, is the actual pitch.

SIGNAL — Early Warning Intelligence for Commercial Credit

A four-person capstone built with an actual financial-services partner. SIGNAL reads credit memos, borrower financial statements, and correspondence, then flags early warning indicators in commercial loan portfolios before they turn into losses — surfacing evidence and a confidence score for a human analyst to confirm, never an automated verdict.

  • Four-stage pipeline — fetch, extract/validate, rules-plus-ML analysis, and a review dashboard — each stage owned separately, each with its own runbook.
  • An operations dashboard graded against four real KPIs, including one deliberately marked "not measurable" rather than a number nobody could stand behind.
  • Incident runbooks for extraction failures and missed alerts, with a governance table that separates what's actually implemented from what's still proposed.
